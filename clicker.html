SQL   /   Retention analysis

Shop Clicker customer retention

Five MySQL queries use purchase history, feedback and engagement gaps to identify customers for follow-up.

MYSQL   /   PURCHASE FREQUENCY

SELECT c.customer_id,
  COUNT(p.purchase_id)
FROM customers c
LEFT JOIN purchases p
  ON c.customer_id = p.customer_id
GROUP BY c.customer_id

Business question

Who shows early signs of disengagement, and what evidence supports a follow-up?

My approach

I designed five related tables for a fictional shop and linked customer, product, purchase, interaction and feedback records. The queries use joins, grouped counts and date windows to identify customers who bought infrequently or stopped interacting.

What I found

The SQL separates customers with an old interaction from those with no recorded interaction. That distinction gives a retention team a more useful starting point for outreach.

The database uses fictional customers. The flags describe observed inactivity in the sample and are intended for review.
